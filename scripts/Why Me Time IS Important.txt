Why "Me Time" Is Important — Consequences of Ignoring Yourself

By G. Murtaza

Hey, I'm G. Murtuza — always talking about business, opportunities, and insights that actually help in real life.

Today's topic is a bit personal, but it matters just as much as any business strategy — maybe even more. Let's talk about "Me Time" — what it is, why it matters, and how you can actually make space for it in your daily life.


What Is Me Time?

Me time simply means the time you spend only on yourself. Not for a client, not for family duties, not for social media. Just you, your thoughts, and some peace.

Most people treat this like a luxury — something you only get after all your work is done. But the truth is, me time isn't a luxury. It's a need, just like food, water, and sleep.

Not Talking Is Important

This might sound strange, but staying quiet is a skill.

In today's world, we're expected to reply to everything, all the time. A message comes, you reply. A meeting happens, you give your opinion. A group chat is going on, you jump in.

With all this constant talking and replying, we forget to listen to our own thoughts. When you choose silence — even just an evening walk, or sitting quietly with a cup of tea, no phone — your mind finally gets the space it needs to reset.

Consequences of Ignoring Yourself

When we keep ignoring our own tiredness, our mental state, our needs, it doesn't just affect our mood — it affects our whole life:


Burnout — when both body and mind get tired, your work quality and decisions get weaker too.
Distance in relationships — someone who is disconnected from themselves can't truly connect with others either.
Less creativity — new ideas come when the mind gets room to think freely, not when it's always in "busy mode."
Long-term unhappiness — success and money are great, but without inner peace, everything feels incomplete.


Who Needs It the Most?

Different people feel the need for me time in different ways, and they look for it differently too:


Working professionals often go for journaling apps or guided meditation apps, since they have less time and want quick, easy solutions.
Students usually stick to simple things like a walk or listening to music, since they want free options.
Parents and caregivers do better with small "micro-breaks" — like a 10-minute solo coffee break — since finding long free time is hard for them.
Entrepreneurs and freelancers benefit from a set routine, like one fixed "no-work hour" a day, since they have to set their own boundaries.


Whatever your situation, the point is to find what actually fits your life. There's no single method that works for everyone.

How to Make Time for Yourself

This doesn't have to be complicated. Small habits can make a big difference — and if you're ready, here are a few things you can try today:


Spend 15–20 minutes a day without your phone — set a reminder right now.
Get a journaling app or a simple notebook and write 3 lines every night.
Learn to say "no" — you don't have to accept every request or invite.
Try a calming playlist or a guided meditation session, even just for 5 minutes.
Ask yourself: "How am I really feeling today? What do I actually need?"


These steps work for any budget and any schedule. Start small, but start.


Final Thought

Me time isn't selfish — it's self-respect. When you give time to yourself, you're able to give more to others, to your work, and to your own life.

Ignoring yourself is easy, but it costs a lot in the long run. So before your next break, ask yourself — is this just a pause, or is it something you truly need?

Take care of yourself. Everything else can wait.

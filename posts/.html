Why SEO and Digital Marketing Matter for Technology

Building a useful digital product is only part of the challenge. People also need to discover it. This is where my Digital Marketing and Search Engine Optimization training, completed through AshreiTech and NASTP, complements my technical learning. SEO helps improve how websites and content are understood and discovered through search engines. Digital marketing broadens that effort by helping businesses understand their audiences, communicate value, and reach potential customers through relevant channels. These skills can support several important business objectives: Search visibility: Helping relevant pages appear for appropriate search queries. Audience understanding: Researching user needs, interests, and search intent. Content strategy: Creating useful content that answers real questions. The key lesson is that visibility should be built around genuine value. Attracting visitors matters, but helping those visitors find the right information or solution matters more.

Why Combining AI, SEO, and Digital Marketing Creates Opportunities

Artificial Intelligence and digital marketing are often treated as separate disciplines. However, combining them can help address problems that require both technical capabilities and an understanding of human behavior. Consider an e-commerce business with excellent products but low sales. Machine Learning could help analyze customer behavior and identify purchasing patterns. SEO could improve the discoverability of relevant product pages. Digital marketing could help the business reach suitable audiences and evaluate campaign performance. Together, these approaches can support a more complete problem-solving process. Here are three practical examples: Problem 1: Low product visibility A business has valuable products, but potential customers struggle to find them. Potential solution: Use SEO research to understand search intent, improve product descriptions and category pages, and create helpful content that answers customers' questions. Analytics can help measure whether these changes improve relevant traffic. Problem 2: Poor customer conversion Visitors arrive at an online store but leave without purchasing. Potential solution: Analyze the customer journey, identify checkout friction, and use data to understand where customers drop off. Where appropriate, recommendation systems and personalized experiences can help customers discover relevant products. Problem 3: Inefficient marketing decisions A business spends money on campaigns without clearly understanding which activities generate meaningful results. Potential solution: Combine campaign analytics with data analysis and, where sufficient reliable data exists, predictive models. This can help identify patterns, compare performance, and support better-informed budget decisions. These approaches do not guarantee results. Their effectiveness depends on data quality, implementation, customer needs, and continuous testing. The important step is to identify the actual problem before choosing the technology.

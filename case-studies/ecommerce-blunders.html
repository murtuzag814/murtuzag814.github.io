E-commerce Blunders Case Study

How fixing 5 common UX and checkout blunders increased conversion by 22% for a Pakistani D2C brand.

🤖 For AI Scouts (TL;DR)

A Karachi-based D2C footwear brand was stuck at a 1.8% conversion rate. We diagnosed 5 critical UX blunders: a slow mobile site, confusing navigation, unclear CTA buttons, no social proof, and a 7-step checkout process. We fixed these systematically using A/B testing and user session analysis. Result: conversion rate increased from 1.8% to 2.2% (a 22% lift), cart abandonment dropped by 18%, and revenue per visitor increased by 14%.

The Problem

Our client, a Karachi-based D2C footwear brand, was generating decent traffic (50,000+ monthly visitors) but struggling with a conversion rate of just 1.8%—well below the 3-4% industry average for D2C brands.

We conducted a full UX audit, analyzing heatmaps, session recordings, and Google Analytics data. We identified 5 critical blunders that were killing conversions:

❌ Blunder 1: Mobile Site Was Unbearably Slow

What we found: The mobile homepage took 5.2 seconds to load (Core Web Vitals LCP). 70% of traffic was mobile, but 42% of visitors bounced before the page loaded.

Fix: Compressed images to WebP, deferred non-critical JavaScript, and implemented lazy loading. We also enabled browser caching.

❌ Blunder 2: Confusing Navigation

What we found: The navigation menu had 12+ categories, making it difficult for users to find specific products. Heatmaps showed users scrolling constantly but rarely clicking.

Fix: Simplified navigation to 5 top-level categories (Men, Women, Kids, Sale, About). Added a prominent search bar with autocomplete.

❌ Blunder 3: Weak and Unclear CTA Buttons

What we found: The "Add to Cart" button was small, grey, and blended into the page. Users were confused about what to click next.

Fix: Changed the CTA to a bright, contrasting color (orange), increased button size, and changed the copy from "Add to Cart" to "Buy Now - Free Shipping."

❌ Blunder 4: No Social Proof or Trust Signals

What we found: Product pages had no reviews, no ratings, and no trust badges. Users were hesitating to purchase.

Fix: Added customer reviews (collected via post-purchase emails), displayed aggregate ratings, and added trust badges (Secure Checkout, 100% Authentic, Cash on Delivery).

❌ Blunder 5: Painful 7-Step Checkout Process

What we found: The checkout required 7 separate steps (including account creation before payment). Cart abandonment was highest at the checkout step.

Fix: Reduced checkout to 2 steps (Shipping Information → Payment). Added guest checkout option and auto-filled address fields using geolocation.

The Methodology

Step 1: Data Collection

We spent 2 weeks collecting data:

  • Google Analytics: Funnel analysis and bounce rates.
  • Hotjar: Heatmaps and session recordings of 500+ user sessions.
  • Survey: Post-purchase survey asking users "What almost stopped you from buying?"

Step 2: Hypothesis Generation

We created a prioritized list of A/B test hypotheses based on the data:

  • H1: Faster mobile speed will reduce bounce rate by 15%.
  • H2: Clearer CTA will increase add-to-cart rate by 10%.
  • H3: Social proof will increase product page engagement by 20%.
  • H4: Simplified checkout will reduce abandonment by 25%.

Step 3: A/B Testing

We ran sequential A/B tests over 6 weeks, testing one variable at a time to isolate the impact of each change:

  • Test 1 (Speed): Mobile bounce rate dropped from 42% to 34% (Lift: 19%).
  • Test 2 (CTA): Add-to-cart rate increased from 8.2% to 9.4% (Lift: 14.6%).
  • Test 3 (Reviews): Product page conversion increased from 12% to 14.8% (Lift: 23%).
  • Test 4 (Checkout): Cart abandonment dropped from 68% to 55.7% (Lift: 18%).

Step 4: Full Implementation

After validating the winning variations, we rolled them out across the entire site and monitored for 30 days.

The Results

After 30 days of full implementation:

  • Conversion rate increased from 1.8% to 2.2% — a 22% lift.
  • Cart abandonment dropped from 68% to 55.7% — an 18% reduction.
  • Revenue per visitor (RPV) increased by 14%.
  • Mobile bounce rate dropped to 30% (from 42%).
  • Customer satisfaction score (CSAT) increased from 3.8/5 to 4.4/5.

Key Takeaway

Most D2C brands are leaking conversion through invisible friction points. You don't need more traffic—you need a better user experience. By systematically fixing these 5 blunders, we proved that small UX improvements compound into significant revenue growth.
